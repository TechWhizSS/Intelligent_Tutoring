1. Introduction to the Course 
1.1 Importance of the Course 
1.1.1 Domain Relevance:  
This course helps students to understand fundamental principles of 
Electrical Engineering, also it provides an overview of evolution of 
electronics, and introduces the working principle and examples of electronic 
devices and circuits to make students understand the impact of technology 
in a global and societal context. 
 
1.1.2 Industry Relevance:  
Electrical and electronics engineering is at the forefront of developing new 
technologies for several industries including transport, healthcare, construction, and 
robotics. With the rapid expansion of the technology sector, the demand for those 
who can create, understand, and alter electrical control systems has risen. Electrical 
and electronic engineers create and improve systems to benefit society. Electrical 
Course Policy  Basic Electrical and Electronics Engineering 
 
design can become a very complex topic, especially where large installations are 
concerned (such as energy grids) or even within highly advanced pieces of small 
hardware (such as those used in smartphones). Therefore, if to be able to work upon 
such systems, a solid understanding of basic circuit design is required which is offered 
by this course. 
1.2 Objectives of the Course: 
1.2.1 To equip the students with the ability to solve, assemble   and test 
simple DC electrical circuits 
1.2.2 To equip the students with the ability to solve, assemble   and test 
simple AC electrical circuits 
1.2.3 To obtain a basic understanding of the working principle and 
applications of electronics devices

4.1 Internal Continuous Assessment (ICA) – 50 marks 
Assessment of ICA comprises the following components. 
1. Class test 1 and 2 
 
a. Two class tests will be conducted as per the academic calendar. 
b. It may be conducted for 20 marks each and scaled down to 10 marks each. 
 
2. Lab Submissions (15 marks) 
 
a. Lab experiments (5 marks) 
i. Continuous assessment for laboratory experiments will be 
conducted. There are 10 practicals, each carrying 
weightage of 10 marks.At the end of the course, average of 
total marks will be taken to obtain marks out of 10 and then scaled 
down to 5. 
ii. Discussion of your work with your peers is allowed. However, each 
student is expected to submit his/her original work. Submissions 
which are very similar will be marked zero. Assessment of the lab 
work will be carried out based on parameters like timely 
completion of lab work file, understanding of the experiment 
performed, originality in the work, involvement of the student, 
regularity, discipline etc. during the session.

3. Assignment (5 marks)-Tutorials will be given to the students 
consisting of mostly numerical problems for home assignment or 
classwork submission. 
4. Implementation of a challenging problem (group activity) (5

Faculty will make group of 2-3 students. 
 
i. Identify a project based on some topic relevant to the course 
ii. Course faculty will approval the project topic 
iii. Request students to implement it on any software platform 
(Multisim, Tinker Cad etc.) 
iv. Assessment will be based on the methodology used to implement 
and understanding of the project. 
 
4.2  Term End Examination (TEE) – (100 marks scaled down to 50) 
A written examination of 100 marks for three hours’ duration will be conducted for 
the course as per the academic calendar. 
Marks obtained scaled down to 50. There will be 7 questions (each question will be 
20 marks). Q1 will be compulsory and any 4 from the remaining to be solved.
